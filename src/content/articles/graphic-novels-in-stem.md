For decades, graphic novels were contraband in the classroom: confiscated, stigmatized, and filed under "not real reading." That framing is finally collapsing, and STEM classrooms are exactly where the collapse is most interesting.

## Visuals are the native language of science

A diagram of the water cycle, a free-body diagram, a graph of enzyme activity — science is already a visual discipline. Graphic novels simply meet students where the discipline already lives. When a cell division process plays out across sequential panels, students track cause and effect the same way a researcher would.

## What teachers report

- **Higher engagement** — reluctant readers finish entire books
- **Better retention** — visual narratives create memory anchors
- **Richer discussion** — panels give every student something concrete to point at

## Getting started

You don't need a curriculum overhaul. Start with one text connected to one unit. Let students read first and analyze second. The rigor comes from what they do with the story — modeling, questioning, rebuilding the science behind the panels.

The medium isn't the message. It's the on-ramp.
